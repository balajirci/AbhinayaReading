Abhinaya's Reading Practice Pack 2

Weeks 5-8. Same 15-minute rhythm as Pack 1, with two new games built from her school board CCAT results. Back to Weeks 1-4

What's new in Pack 2 - and why

Her CCAT results (school board test, October 2026) agreed with the Oxford assessment - and sharpened two things:

  • Verbal was her lowest battery (24th percentile). The CCAT verbal battery is not just about knowing words - it tests how words relate to each other: groups, opposites, and patterns. So Pack 2 adds Word Detective: analogies ("puppy is to dog as kitten is to...?"), sorting words into groups, and finishing sentences with the word that makes sense - and saying why.
  • Quantitative was her strongest battery (68th percentile, High Average). Pack 2 uses that strength as the engine: Number Stories are reading passages built around numbers, time, and money, so her best skill carries her weakest one. They also quietly practise the topics Oxford flagged as new for Grade 3 - multiplication as groups, sharing as division, telling time, and coins - inside stories, not worksheets.
  • Passages are a little longer than Pack 1, and each week still ends with a Brave Guess question - a star for any guess with a reason.

Rhythm: one activity a day, any order, about 15 minutes. Five activities a week now - Word Power, Word Detective, Read and Talk, Listen Up, and Number Story (which ends with one quick sentence stretch, so writing practice continues). Same confidence rules as Pack 1: praise attempts, never correct mid-sentence, stop while it is still fun.

Week 5 - The Garden

Word Power Odd one out

  1. seed - leaf - root - rock
  2. water - pour - drink - juice (tricky - talk it through)
  3. spring - summer - autumn - birthday
  4. dig - plant - grow - eat (tricky - which one does the plant not do?)
Check answers

1) rock - the others are parts of a plant. 2) Tricky: water, drink and juice are all things you can drink; pour is an action. Accept a reasoned case for juice too (it is a drink, water can be a verb). The talking is the point. 3) birthday - the others are seasons. 4) eat - a plant is dug around, planted, and grows, but it does not eat (not the way animals do).

Words to own this week

sprout
When a seed first starts to grow and a tiny green shoot pops out. Make a sentence: "The seed sprouted after..."
soil
The dark earth plants grow in (another word for dirt, but gardeners say soil). Make a sentence: "The soil was wet because..."
weed
A wild plant growing where you do not want it. Tricky word: it can also be an action - "to weed the garden" means pulling weeds out. Talk: Use it both ways in two sentences.
bloom
When a flower opens up. Make a sentence: "The roses bloomed in..."
harvest (HAR-vest)
Picking or gathering the food that has grown. Make a sentence: "In autumn, we harvest..."

Word Detective How do words connect?

Finish the pattern (say the missing word, then say WHY it fits):

  1. Puppy is to dog as kitten is to ______.
  2. Seed is to plant as egg is to ______.
  3. Day is to sun as night is to ______.
  4. Shovel is to digging as watering can is to ______.

Sort the words: Put these 9 words into 3 groups of 3, and give each group a name: carrot, rose, apple, tulip, banana, potato, sunflower, grape, onion.

Finish the sentence - pick the word that makes sense and say why: "The gardener pulled out the weeds so the flowers would have room to ______." (grow / sleep / hide)

Check answers

Patterns: 1) cat (baby animal to grown animal). 2) bird or chick (what hatches/grows from it). 3) moon (what shines in it). 4) watering (tool to its job). Sort: flowers - rose, tulip, sunflower; fruits - apple, banana, grape; vegetables - carrot, potato, onion. Sentence: grow.

Read and Talk The Seed That Waited

A tiny seed lay in the dark soil all winter. Above it, snow covered the garden. "Nothing is happening," thought the seed. But deep inside, it was getting ready.

In spring, the rain came, drip by drip. The soil grew warm. One morning, the seed split open, and a pale little root pushed down into the earth. A green shoot pushed up, looking for the sun.

All summer the plant grew taller. Bees visited its flowers. By autumn, there were five round, red tomatoes hanging from its stems - and inside every tomato were dozens of new seeds, waiting for their turn.

Retell first: "Tell me the seed's whole year - winter, spring, summer, autumn."

  1. What two things did the seed push out in spring? (literal)
  2. What grew on the plant by autumn? (literal)
  3. The seed thought "nothing is happening" in winter. Was it right? How do you know? (inference - Brave Guess)

Listen Up Ears only

Parent reads aloud, once: "Grandma grows tomatoes in her garden behind the house. This year she planted eight tomato plants in two neat rows. Every morning she checks them with her watering can. The first tomato turned red on a Tuesday. Grandma picked it, cut it into four pieces, and shared it with the whole family. 'The first one always tastes the best,' she said."

  1. How many tomato plants did Grandma plant?
  2. On what day did the first tomato turn red?
  3. Into how many pieces did she cut it?

Sentence echo game:

  1. "The garden is green."
  2. "In spring, the quiet garden turns green and busy."
  3. "In spring, the quiet garden turns green and busy, with bees humming over the flowers."

Number Story Planting Day

On Saturday morning, Leo's class planted a school garden. They dug 4 rows, and in each row they planted 6 seeds. "That is 4 groups of 6," said their teacher. Leo watered the first row at 9 o'clock. He watered the last row at half past 9, because the watering can kept running empty. At the end of the day, the class hung up a sign: "Please do not step here. Something is growing."

  1. How many seeds did the class plant in all? (Find it with the story's own clue: 4 groups of 6. Counting by sixes is allowed - or draw the rows.)
  2. Leo started watering at 9 o'clock and finished at half past 9. Was that a long time or a short time? What slowed him down?
  3. Sentence stretch: Start with "The class planted seeds." Add: How many? Where? Why?

Week 6 - Trains and Travel

Word Power Odd one out

  1. train - bus - bicycle - elephant
  2. arrive - leave - depart - go away (tricky)
  3. ticket - seat - window - journey (tricky)
  4. near - close - beside - far
Check answers

1) elephant - the others are ways to travel. 2) arrive - the others all mean going away; arrive means coming in. 3) journey - the others are things you can point to on a train; a journey is the whole trip. 4) far - the others mean near.

Words to own this week

journey
A trip, especially a long one. Make a sentence: "Our journey to ______ took..."
platform
The raised area at a station where you wait for and board the train. Make a sentence: "We stood on the platform and..."
arrive
To get to the place you were going to. Talk: What is the opposite of arrive? (depart/leave)
depart
To leave a place. Trains depart, planes depart. Make a sentence: "The train departs at..."
sparkle (review)
From Week 2. Make a sentence: "The train windows sparkled when..."

Word Detective

Finish the pattern:

  1. Wheel is to car as wing is to ______.
  2. Arrive is to depart as come is to ______.
  3. Driver is to bus as pilot is to ______.
  4. Ticket is to train as key is to ______.

Sort the words: 3 groups of 3, name each group: station, backpack, map, platform, suitcase, ticket office, lunchbox, conductor's booth... - use these 9: station, backpack, map, platform, suitcase, signal, lunchbox, tracks, camera.

Finish the sentence: "We ran to the platform because the train was about to ______." (depart / arrive to our house / sleep) - say why.

Check answers

Patterns: 1) airplane (or bird - accept with a reason; the part that helps it move through air). 2) go. 3) airplane. 4) door or lock (the thing it opens/lets you into - a ticket lets you onto the train). Sort (one good answer): places at a station - station, platform, tracks; things you pack - backpack, suitcase, lunchbox; things that help or show - map, signal, camera. Accept other groupings with sensible names. Sentence: depart.

Read and Talk The Night Train

The train left the city just as the sky turned orange. Nila pressed her nose to the window and watched the buildings slide away. Soon there were fields, then a river shining in the last light.

When it grew dark, the conductor came down the aisle punching tickets. "Two more hours," he said. Nila's family ate their dinner from a lunchbox - sandwiches, grapes, and juice - while the train rocked gently side to side, clickety-clack, clickety-clack.

Nila tried hard to stay awake to see the stars. But the rocking of the train was like a lullaby, and before the moon was fully up, she was fast asleep, curled against her mother's shoulder.

Retell first: "Tell me the journey from the start to the end."

  1. What did the family eat for dinner on the train? (literal)
  2. How long did the conductor say was left in the journey? (literal)
  3. Why do you think Nila fell asleep even though she tried to stay awake? Point to the words that tell you. (inference - Brave Guess)

Listen Up

Parent reads aloud, once, like a station announcement: "Attention, please. The train for the city will leave from Platform 3 in ten minutes. Passengers should have their tickets ready. The journey takes two hours, and the train will stop twice on the way - first at the lake town, then at the airport station. Please do not leave your bags alone. Thank you, and have a safe journey."

  1. From which platform does the train leave?
  2. How long does the journey take, and how many stops are there?
  3. What two places does the train stop at?

Follow my instructions (no repeating):

  1. "Point to the door, then point to the window."
  2. "Clap once, then point to the ceiling, then say the word 'train'."
  3. "Say the word 'ticket', then clap twice, then point to the door, then sit down slowly like a train stopping."

Number Story Tickets, Please!

The little train to the lake leaves at 4 o'clock. The trip takes 2 hours. Sam's ticket cost 2 dollars, and his sister's ticket cost 2 dollars too. Their dad paid with a 5-dollar bill. "First we stop at the lake town," said the conductor, "and we get there at 5 o'clock - right in the middle of the trip." Sam counted the passengers in his car: 9 people. Then 3 more got on at the lake town, and nobody got off.

  1. What time will the train reach the end of the trip? (Leaving at 4, trip takes 2 hours.)
  2. Two tickets at 2 dollars each, paid with 5 dollars. How much change should Dad get?
  3. After the lake town stop, how many passengers are in Sam's car? Sentence stretch: Start with "The train stopped." Add: Where? Who got on? How many were there now?

Week 7 - Games and Sports

Word Power Odd one out

  1. kick - throw - catch - read
  2. win - lose - tie - prize (tricky)
  3. first - second - third - banana (easy laugh - take it)
  4. team - player - coach - chessboard (tricky)
Check answers

1) read - the others are ball actions. 2) prize - the others are ways a game can end; a prize is a thing. 3) banana - the others are positions in a race. 4) chessboard - the others are people.

Words to own this week

practice
Doing something again and again to get better at it. Tricky word: it can be an action ("I practice piano") and a thing ("piano practice"). Talk: Use it both ways.
score
The number of points in a game. Also an action: "She scored a goal." Make two sentences: one for each meaning.
cheer
To shout happily to support someone. Make a sentence: "The crowd cheered when..."
trophy (TRO-fee)
A prize, often a shiny cup, for winning. Make a sentence: "The trophy went to..."
brave (review)
From Week 1. Talk: How can someone be brave in a game, even when they lose?

Word Detective

Finish the pattern:

  1. Glove is to hand as sock is to ______.
  2. Win is to lose as happy is to ______.
  3. First is to last as top is to ______.
  4. Kick is to soccer as hit with a bat is to ______.

Sort the words: 3 groups of 3, name each group: chess, swimming, checkers, running, cards, jumping, soccer... - use these 9: chess, swimming, checkers, running, card games, jumping, hide and seek, tag, hopscotch. (Hint: think about WHERE each one is played.)

Finish the sentence: "Even though her team lost the game, Ana smiled because ______." (she had tried her best / the scoreboard broke / she did not play) - pick and say why.

Check answers

Patterns: 1) foot. 2) sad. 3) bottom. 4) baseball (or cricket - accept either with a reason). Sort (one good answer): indoor/table games - chess, checkers, card games; water - swimming (a group of one is suspicious - encourage regrouping); playground/outdoor - running, jumping, hide and seek, tag, hopscotch. The honest lesson: sorting can be done more than one way; her job is to defend her groups. Sentence: she had tried her best.

Read and Talk The Game Priya Lost

Priya was learning chess. Her big brother had taught her how the pieces move, and now she wanted to win a real game. On Saturday, she played against the boy next door, who had been playing for two years.

Priya thought hard about every move. Twice she almost captured his queen. But slowly, one by one, her pieces disappeared from the board. At the end, the boy smiled and said, "Checkmate."

Priya's eyes stung a little. But then she looked at the board again. She had lasted much longer than last time, and she had almost caught his queen - twice. "Same time next week?" she asked. "I am going to figure out how you did that."

Retell first: "Tell me what happened, and how Priya felt at the start, the middle, and the end."

  1. Who taught Priya to play chess, and who did she play against? (literal)
  2. How do you know Priya played better than her last game, even though she lost? (inference - Brave Guess)
  3. What do you think Priya will do before next week's game? What in the story tells you? (inference)

Listen Up

Parent reads aloud, once (a new game - she must listen to understand the rules): "Here is a game called Statue Tag. One person is the Keeper. Everyone else runs around. When the Keeper shouts 'Freeze!', everyone must stand as still as a statue. If you move, you sit down. The last statue standing becomes the next Keeper. But there is one trick: the Keeper can try to make you laugh - without touching you."

  1. What happens when the Keeper shouts "Freeze!"?
  2. What happens if you move?
  3. What is the one trick the Keeper is allowed to use?

Sentence echo game:

  1. "She played a good game."
  2. "Even though she lost, she played a very good game."
  3. "Even though she lost the game on Saturday, she played better than ever and asked for a rematch."

Number Story The Scoreboard

The schoolyard soccer final was tied. The Red team had scored 2 goals in the first half, and the Blue team had scored 2 goals too. In the second half, Blue scored 1 more goal. Then, in the very last minute, Red scored 2 goals - one right after the other! The referee blew the whistle 3 times to end the game, and both teams shook hands. The trophy would be shared: Red keeps it for 6 months, then Blue keeps it for 6 months.

  1. What was the final score? (Add up each team's goals carefully.)
  2. Red keeps the trophy for 6 months, then Blue for 6 months. After one full year, whose turn is it again? Sentence stretch: Start with "Red won the game." Add: By how much? How did it happen?

Week 8 - Fix It and Build It

Word Power Odd one out

  1. hammer - screwdriver - wrench - banana (a classic - take the laugh)
  2. build - fix - break - make (tricky)
  3. wheel - door - roof - window (one is not part of a house)
  4. loud - quiet - silent - still (tricky - talk it through)
Check answers

1) banana - the others are tools. 2) break - the others mean putting together or repairing. 3) wheel - the others are parts of a house. 4) Tricky: quiet, silent and still can all mean no sound/movement; loud is the safe answer (the other three are its opposites). Accept a reasoned case for still (it is about movement, not sound).

Words to own this week

invent
To make something completely new that nobody made before. Make a sentence: "One day I want to invent..."
machine
A thing with parts that move together to do a job - a bicycle is a simple machine, a car is a bigger one. Make a sentence: "The machine helps us by..."
solve
To figure out the answer to a problem. Make a sentence: "We solved the problem by..."
tool
Something you hold in your hand to help you do a job. Talk: Name a tool for cooking, a tool for writing, and a tool for fixing.
journey (review)
From Week 6. Talk: Inventing something can be a journey too - what would that mean?

Word Detective

Finish the pattern:

  1. Pen is to writing as hammer is to ______.
  2. Big is to small as broken is to ______.
  3. Doctor is to hospital as teacher is to ______.
  4. Problem is to solve as question is to ______.

Sort the words: 3 groups of 3, name each group: scissors, crayon, glue, ruler, hammer, pencil, saw, eraser, screwdriver.

Finish the sentence: "The lamp would not turn on, so Dad checked whether it was ______." (plugged in / under the bed / singing) - say why.

Check answers

Patterns: 1) hammering / building / fixing (tool to its job). 2) fixed (or whole/mended). 3) school. 4) answer. Sort (one good answer): writing and drawing - crayon, pencil, eraser... note eraser 'fixes' writing; cutting and sticking - scissors, glue; measuring and building - ruler, hammer, saw, screwdriver. Again, defendable groupings win. Sentence: plugged in.

Read and Talk Nila and the Broken Bicycle

Nila loved her bicycle, but one morning the chain had slipped off, and the back wheel would not turn. Her father said, "We can take it to the repair shop on Saturday." Saturday felt very far away.

So Nila looked closely. She turned the bicycle upside down, the way she had seen the repair man do it. Slowly, she worked the chain back onto the gear with a spoon handle, turning the pedal a little at a time. It took three tries, and her hands got greasy and black.

Then she climbed on and pedalled down the driveway. The wheel turned smoothly. "You solved it," said her father. Nila grinned. "I just didn't stop trying."

Retell first: "Tell me the problem, what Nila tried, and how it ended."

  1. What was wrong with the bicycle? (literal)
  2. What did Nila use to work the chain back on? (literal)
  3. Nila's father said "You solved it." What does her answer - "I just didn't stop trying" - tell you about how problems get solved? (inference - Brave Guess)

Listen Up

Parent reads aloud, once: "Long ago, people read at night by candlelight. Then a man named Thomas Edison tried thousands of ideas to make a light bulb that would last. Many of his tries failed. But he kept careful notes about every single try, so he never made the same mistake twice. When the bulb finally worked, it stayed bright for more than thirteen hours. People said he was a genius. Edison said it was mostly hard work."

  1. What did people use for light before the light bulb?
  2. What did Edison write down after every try?
  3. How long did the first successful bulb stay bright?

Follow my instructions (no repeating):

  1. "Pretend to turn a screwdriver, then stop."
  2. "Pretend to hammer a nail twice, then pretend to turn a screwdriver, then stop."
  3. "Point to something in this room that is a tool, then pretend to use it, then say one job it does."

Number Story The Cookie Boxes

For the school fair, Ana baked 24 cookies. She packed them into boxes of 4 cookies each. Her brother said, "That is 24 shared into groups of 4." Every box cost 3 dollars. Her friend bought one box and paid with a 5-dollar bill. By lunch, Ana had sold 5 boxes, and exactly one box was left on the table.

  1. How many boxes did Ana pack in all? (Check your answer against the story: she sold 5 and 1 was left.)
  2. Her friend paid 5 dollars for a 3-dollar box. How much change did Ana give back? Sentence stretch: Start with "Ana sold the cookies." Add: How many? For how much? What was left?
