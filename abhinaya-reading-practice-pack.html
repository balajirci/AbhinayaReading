Abhinaya's Reading Practice Pack

Short daily practice at home, built from her October 2026 assessment: strong word reading, growing word meanings, listening, and brave guessing.

How to use this pack

15 minutes a day, 4 or 5 days a week. Each week has one of each activity. Do one activity a day, in any order. Short and finished beats long and perfect.

  • Word Power - word meanings: the biggest gap (tested Grade 1) and the top priority.
  • Read and Talk - a short passage she reads, then retells in her own words before questions.
  • Listen Up - you read aloud; she only listens. Builds listening memory and comprehension.
  • Build a Sentence - playful sentence writing with target words (Grade 2 level).

The confidence rules (these matter as much as the exercises)

  • Brave Guess stars: she earns a star for ANY guess, right or wrong. Guessing is the skill being trained. Say: "I love that you guessed. Let's check together."
  • Never correct mid-sentence. Let her finish, praise the attempt, then revisit.
  • Retell first, questions second. A wobbly retell with effort beats a perfect answer read off the page.
  • Stop while it is still fun. End on a win, even if that means cutting an activity short.
  • No timers, no speed. She already reads words above grade level - this pack is about meaning, not speed.

Answers for parents are tucked under "Check answers" - tap or click to open. Weeks 1-4 below; the tracker at the end shows what progress looks like.

Week 1 - Animals

Word Power Odd one out

Read the four words together. She picks the one that does not belong and says why. Any sensible reason counts.

  1. apple - banana - carrot - grapes
  2. dog - cat - horse - table
  3. whisper - shout - yell - scream
  4. tiny - small - little - enormous
Check answers

1) carrot - it is a vegetable, the others are fruits. 2) table - it is not an animal. 3) whisper - it is quiet, the others are loud. 4) enormous - it means very BIG, the others mean small.

Words to own this week

enormous (ee-NOR-mus)
Very, very big. Make a sentence: "The elephant is enormous because..."
gentle
Soft and kind, not rough. Make a sentence: "Be gentle with..."
sneaky
Quiet and secret, so no one notices. Make a sentence: "The sneaky cat..."
brave
Doing something even when it feels scary. Make a sentence: "I was brave when..."
gigantic
Huge - even bigger than enormous? Talk: Which is bigger, enormous or gigantic? (Trick question - both mean very big. Any answer with a reason wins.)

Read and Talk The Fox and the Grapes

A hungry fox saw a bunch of grapes hanging high on a vine. The grapes looked sweet and juicy. The fox jumped once. He jumped twice. He jumped and jumped, but the grapes were too high.

At last the fox walked away. "Those grapes are probably sour anyway," he said. But deep down, he still wished he could taste them.

Retell first: "Tell me the story in your own words - what happened first, next, and last?"

  1. What did the fox want? (literal)
  2. Why could he not get the grapes? (literal)
  3. The fox said the grapes were sour. Do you think he really believed that? How do you know? (inference - the Brave Guess question)

Listen Up Ears only - no peeking at the words

Parent reads aloud, once, slowly: "Mia has a pet turtle named Pebbles. Pebbles is small and green, and he moves very, very slowly. Every morning, Mia gives Pebbles a piece of lettuce. Pebbles loves lettuce more than anything. One day, Mia could not find Pebbles anywhere. She looked under the sofa and behind the door. At last she found him sleeping inside her shoe."

  1. What is the turtle's name?
  2. What does Pebbles eat every morning?
  3. Where did Mia finally find him?

Sentence echo game: read a sentence, she repeats it exactly. Start short, grow slowly. Stop at the first one she misses, celebrate, done.

  1. "The cat is sleeping."
  2. "The brown cat is sleeping on the mat."
  3. "The small brown cat is sleeping on the warm mat by the door."

Build a Sentence

Use each word in a sentence of her own. Silly is welcome. Write them down for her if handwriting slows her down - the thinking is the exercise.

  1. Make a sentence with gentle.
  2. Make a sentence with sneaky.
  3. Stretch it: Start with "The dog ran." Add words to answer: What dog? Ran where? Ran why? Build the longest sentence you can together.

Week 2 - Sky and Space

Word Power Odd one out

  1. sun - moon - star - banana
  2. hot - warm - boiling - freezing
  3. fast - quick - speedy - slow
  4. above - below - under - beneath
Check answers

1) banana - it is food, the others are in the sky. 2) freezing - it means very cold, the others mean hot. 3) slow - the others mean fast. 4) above - it means over, the others all mean under.

Words to own this week

float
To rest on top of water or drift in the air. Make a sentence: "The balloon floated..."
shadow
The dark shape made when something blocks the light. Make a sentence: "My shadow..."
sparkle
To shine with tiny flashes of light. Make a sentence: "The stars sparkle when..."
vanish
To disappear suddenly. Make a sentence: "The magician made the coin vanish by..."
gigantic (review)
From Week 1. Talk: Name something gigantic in the sky. Name something tiny in the sky.

Read and Talk Why the Moon Changes Shape

Some nights the Moon looks like a round silver coin. Other nights it looks like a banana, or a tiny fingernail clipping in the sky. But the Moon is not really changing. It is always a round ball.

The Sun shines on the Moon, and we can only see the bright part. As the Moon travels around the Earth, we see a little more or a little less of that bright part. That is why the Moon seems to change shape.

Retell first: "Explain it back to me like you are the teacher."

  1. Does the Moon really change shape? (literal)
  2. What shines on the Moon? (literal)
  3. If we see only a tiny sliver of the Moon tonight, where is most of the Moon's bright part? (inference - Brave Guess)

Listen Up

Parent reads aloud, once: "Arun wants to be an astronaut when he grows up. He reads books about rockets and planets. His favourite planet is Saturn because of its beautiful rings. At night, Arun looks at the sky through his window and counts the stars. Last night he counted all the way to fifty before he fell asleep."

  1. What does Arun want to be when he grows up?
  2. Which planet is his favourite, and why?
  3. How many stars did he count last night?

Follow my instructions game (listening memory - do them in order, no repeating):

  1. "Clap twice, then touch your toes."
  2. "Spin around once, then hop like a bunny, then sit down."
  3. "Touch your nose, then clap once, then touch your ears, then stand very still like a statue."

Build a Sentence

  1. Make a sentence with float.
  2. Make a sentence with vanish.
  3. Stretch it: Start with "The star sparkled." Add: Which star? Sparkled where? Who saw it?

Week 3 - In the Kitchen

Word Power Odd one out

  1. spoon - fork - knife - shoe
  2. sweet - sour - salty - purple
  3. mix - stir - pour - sleep
  4. breakfast - lunch - dinner - midnight
Check answers

1) shoe - the others are for eating. 2) purple - it is a colour, the others are tastes. 3) sleep - the others are cooking actions. 4) midnight - it is a time of night, the others are meals.

Words to own this week

delicious
Tastes very, very good. Make a sentence: "The most delicious food is..."
recipe (RESS-uh-pee)
Instructions that tell you how to cook something. Make a sentence: "The recipe says to..."
spill
When liquid falls out by accident. Make a sentence: "Oops! I spilled..."
crunchy
Makes a loud noise when you bite it. Talk: Name two crunchy foods and two soft foods.
gentle (review)
From Week 1. Make a sentence: "Stir the soup gently so..."

Read and Talk The Pancake That Ran Away

One morning, Grandma made a big round pancake. It smelled sweet and warm. Just as she lifted the pan, the pancake jumped out and rolled across the floor!

"Come back!" called Grandma. The pancake rolled past the cat. It rolled past the dog. It rolled out the door and down the path. But at the gate, it met a hungry little bird - and that was the end of the runaway pancake.

Retell first: "What happened first, next, and last?"

  1. Who made the pancake? (literal)
  2. What two animals did the pancake roll past? (literal)
  3. Why do you think the bird stopped the pancake when Grandma could not? (inference - Brave Guess)

Listen Up

Parent reads aloud, once: "For the school bake sale, Ravi and his dad baked muffins on Sunday. They made twelve muffins with blueberries and twelve muffins with chocolate chips. Ravi's little sister ate one chocolate muffin before the sale even started. So they packed twenty-three muffins into a big box and carried them to school on Monday morning."

  1. How many muffins did they bake in all?
  2. What were the two kinds of muffins?
  3. Why did they pack only twenty-three?

Sentence echo game:

  1. "I like warm soup."
  2. "I like warm tomato soup with bread."
  3. "On rainy days, I like warm tomato soup with soft bread and a tall glass of milk."

Build a Sentence

  1. Make a sentence with delicious.
  2. Make a sentence with spill.
  3. Stretch it: Start with "She stirred the soup." Add: Who is she? What soup? Why was she making it?

Week 4 - Ocean and Weather

Word Power Odd one out

  1. rain - snow - wind - sandwich
  2. deep - shallow - low - short (tricky - talk it through)
  3. wave - splash - swim - dry
  4. morning - evening - night - yesterday
Check answers

1) sandwich - the others are weather. 2) Tricky one: in water, deep and shallow are opposites; low and short can describe shallow too. Accept any answer with a good reason - the talking is the point. 3) dry - the others are wet water words. 4) yesterday - the others are parts of today.

Words to own this week

deep
Going far down. Make a sentence: "The ocean is deep, so..."
shallow
Not deep - the water only comes up a little way. Make a sentence: "Babies swim in the shallow end because..."
storm
Wild weather with strong wind and rain. Make a sentence: "During the storm, we..."
collect
To gather things and keep them. Make a sentence: "I collect..."
sparkle (review)
From Week 2. Make a sentence: "The water sparkled when..."

Read and Talk The Hermit Crab Needs a New Shell

A little hermit crab lived in a shell that was getting too small. Every day, the shell pinched a bit more. "I need a bigger home," said the crab.

He searched the beach. The first shell was too big and heavy. The second shell had a crack. The third shell was just right - smooth, strong, and roomy. The crab backed out of his old shell and slipped into the new one. "Perfect," he said, and scuttled happily into the waves.

Retell first: "Tell me the story like you are telling a friend who never heard it."

  1. Why did the crab need a new shell? (literal)
  2. What was wrong with the first two shells? (literal)
  3. How do you think the crab felt in the old shell at the start? What in the story tells you? (inference - Brave Guess)

Listen Up

Parent reads aloud, once: "On Saturday, Sara went to the beach with her grandmother. They built a tall sandcastle with four towers. Sara collected seven shells - three white ones, two pink ones, and two with stripes. When the waves started coming closer, they took a photo of the castle, picked up their bucket, and walked home for lunch."

  1. How many towers did the sandcastle have?
  2. How many shells did Sara collect, and what colours were they?
  3. Why did they leave when they did?

Follow my instructions game:

  1. "Wave your hand, then say your name."
  2. "Pat your head twice, then wave your hand, then say your name."
  3. "Say your name, then pat your head once, then wave twice, then sit down and fold your arms."

Build a Sentence

  1. Make a sentence with collect.
  2. Make a sentence with shallow.
  3. Stretch it: Start with "The wave crashed." Add: Which wave? Crashed where? Who was watching?
